# One long doubling cycle suffices for Fonollosa’s lower bound

Suppose \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) consists of \(n\ge2\) distinct residues. Call \(A\) *valid* if the multiset containing each label once is uniquely determined, among size-\(n\) multisets on \(A\), by its sum. Suppose **some affine doubling map \(a\mapsto2a+t\) has a cycle of length \(r\ge n/2\) entirely in \(A\)**. Then
\[
                           N\ge2^n-2^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}.
\]
There is no assumption on the other \(n-r\) labels. In particular, the whole set need not be closed under any affine doubling map. This proves [Fonollosa’s conjectured bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08366) for the stated class.

Translate by \(t\) so the map on the cycle is ordinary doubling. Write \(C=\{y,2y,\dots,2^{r-1}y\}\), \(B=A\setminus C\), \(k=|B|=n-r\). The subgroup \(H=\langle y\rangle\) has order \(2^r-1\). Indeed, \(2^ry=y\), so \(\operatorname{ord}(y)\) divides \(2^r-1\); a valid \(r\)-term dyadic progression requires order at least \(2^r-r\) by the binary splitting argument in the [classification note](affine-doubling-classification.md), excluding every proper divisor. The case \(r=1\) gives order one directly. In particular \(\sum C=0\).

**The cycle can fill a prescribed difference.** Every nonzero element of \(H\) has a representative \(hy\) with \(1\le h\le2^r-2\). The binary digits of \(h\) express it using between 1 and \(r-1\) cycle coins. A coin may be replaced by two copies of its predecessor anywhere on the cycle, so it has a representation by exactly \(r-1\) coins, or by any greater number. Zero is the sum of one copy of every cycle label and can likewise be represented by exactly \(r\) coins or more.

Let \(\pi:G\to G/H\) be the quotient map. Distinct subsets \(S,T\subseteq B\) cannot have equal projected sums. Orient a hypothetical collision with \(|S|\ge|T|\), and replace \(C\cup S\) by \(T\) and exactly \(r+|S|-|T|\) cycle coins representing \(\sum S-\sum T\). This is a different multiset of the same size and sum. Thus \(q=|G/H|\ge2^k\). If \(q\ge2^k+1\), then since \(r\ge k\),
\[
       N=q(2^r-1)\ge(2^k+1)(2^r-1)
                     =2^n+2^r-2^k-1\ge2^n-1.
\]

**At equality the outside labels form an actual dyadic path.** Suppose \(q=2^k\) with \(k>0\). The projected subset sums now give every quotient element exactly once. For each \(a\in B\) with \(2\pi(a)\ne0\), write \(2\pi(a)=\pi(\sum T)\) in this way. If \(|T|\ge2\), replace \(T\cup C\) by two copies of \(a\) and \(r+|T|-2\) cycle coins, an impossible collision. Since \(2\pi(a)\ne0\), the only possibility is \(T=\{b\}\). If \(b-2a\ne0\), replace \(\{b\}\cup C\) by two copies of \(a\) and \(r-1\) cycle coins of sum \(b-2a\), again impossible. Thus \(b=2a\) **in \(G\)**.

The cyclic group \(G/H\) of order \(2^k\) is generated by the projected labels. At least one \(x\in B\) must project to a generator: otherwise all projected labels are in the index-two subgroup. Repeatedly applying the preceding conclusion to \(x,2x,\dots\) places \(x,2x,\dots,2^{k-1}x\) in \(B\); they are distinct already in the quotient, so they constitute all of \(B\).

If \(2^k-1\ge n\), the binary sum \(1+2+\cdots+2^{k-1}=2^k-1\) could be split to **exactly \(n\) coins**, by splitting a coin larger than one as long as fewer than \(n\) coins remain. Multiplying by \(x\) yields \(n\) outside labels with the same sum as all of \(A\), since \(\sum C=0\). That contradicts validity. Therefore \(2^k\le n\); the same inequality is immediate for \(k=0\). We conclude \(N=2^k(2^r-1)=2^n-2^k\ge2^n-2^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}\).

The quotient counting and the equality-case progression argument do not require \(r\ge k\); that assumption enters only in the displayed estimate when the quotient has at least one unused residue.

**An example beyond closure.** At \(n=8,N=254\), [Sitjar’s computation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24228247) reports two affine equivalence classes. Our independent enumeration gives one represented by \(\{0,1,64,96,112,120,124,126\}\). Under \(a\mapsto2a+126\) all its points except 1 form a seven-cycle. Testing the 254 possible values of \(t\) shows no affine doubling map closes this whole set. Thus the long-cycle hypothesis actually includes examples outside the [closed-set classification](affine-doubling-classification.md).

# A \(7/6\) lower bound for the cyclic minimum modulus

Let \(n\ge3\), and let \(A\subseteq\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z\) have \(n\) elements. Suppose that the multiset containing every element of \(A\) exactly once is the unique multiset of length \(n\) on \(A\) with its sum. Then
\[
                         N\ \ge\ \frac76\,2^{n-1}.
\]
This strengthens the [\(9/8\) estimate](cyclic-surplus.md) by counting gaps of lengths two and three together. The bound applies to arbitrary residue sets, without a search. The conjectured optimum, \(2^n-2^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}\), remains larger.

Translate a pivot to zero and call the other labels \(g_1,\dots,g_{n-1}\). Write \(\sigma(T)=\sum_{j\in T}g_j\). If a multiset \(Y\) of nonpivot labels sums to \(\sigma(T)\) and \(|Y|\le |T|+1\), then \(Y=T\): replace \(T\) by \(Y\) in the original multiset and fill unused positions with zeros. It follows that the subset sums are distinct. Choose \(i\) with \(2g_i\ne0\), possible since a cyclic group has at most two elements killed by 2 and there are at least four subset sums. Put \(g=g_i\), \(S=[n-1]\setminus\{i\}\), \(C=\{\sigma(T):T\subseteq S\}\), \(s=|C|=2^{n-2}\), and \(L=N-2s\). The two sets \(C,C+g\) are disjoint.

**Ties with a common shift.** At most \(s/2\) target masks \(T\subseteq S\) satisfy \(\sigma(T)=2g+\sigma(A')\) for a mask \(A'\subseteq[n-1]\) with \(|A'|=|T|\). The source \(A'\) may contain \(i\). To prove this, cancel common coordinates and put \(U=T\setminus A'\), \(V=A'\setminus T\). Then \(|U|=|V|\), \(U\ne\varnothing\), and \(\sigma(U)-\sigma(V)=2g\). Two distinct reduced pairs satisfy \(U\cap V'\ne\varnothing\) and \(U'\cap V\ne\varnothing\): otherwise one side of \(\sigma(U)+\sigma(V')=\sigma(U')+\sigma(V)\) is squarefree, so equal lengths force equal multisets, hence equal reduced pairs. Each pair constrains a target to a face of the \(S\)-cube: include \(U\) and exclude \(V\cap S\). Both cross-intersections survive projection since \(U,U'\subseteq S\). The faces are proper, and distinct faces differ on at least two fixed coordinates. Such faces cover at most half of a cube: a codimension-one face precludes all others; otherwise slice on any coordinate and apply induction, since each surviving face is still proper.

**Gap count.** In each coset of \(\langle g\rangle\) meeting \(C\), move by steps \(+g\) from one point \(\sigma(B)\in C\) to the next \(\sigma(T)\in C\). If the coset contains just one point of \(C\), take the full circuit. The resulting \(s\) gaps have lengths \(\ell\ge2\) totaling at most \(N\). The word \(B\) followed by \(\ell\) copies of \(i\) has sum \(\sigma(T)\) and differs from \(T\), so its length is at least \(|T|+2\). Thus every gap has nonnegative integer slack \(e=\ell-2+|B|-|T|\). Since both the sources and the targets run through every mask once,
\[
                   \sum e=\sum(\ell-2)\le L.
\]
At most \(L\) gaps have positive slack. At most \(L/2\) have length at least four. For any remaining gap, if \(\ell=2\), take \(A'=B\); if \(\ell=3\), take \(A'=B\cup\{i\}\). In either case \(|A'|=|T|\) and \(\sigma(T)=2g+\sigma(A')\). The tie bound counts those two kinds together, giving \(s\le L+L/2+s/2\). Hence \(L\ge s/3\) and \(N=2s+L\ge(7/6)2^{n-1}\).

**Verification and context.** [CyclicSurplusSeven.lean](CyclicSurplusSeven.lean), theorem `OMA.CyclicSurplus.modulus_seven_lower`, proves this inequality for the upstream `IsValidMod` predicate. The formal proof bounds positive slack by summing a shortest-word potential over the two predecessor steps; for the long-gap bound it counts two distinct sites outside \(C\cup(C+g)\) per target. The [earlier note](cyclic-surplus.md) gives the definitions, the potential argument and references. [Fonollosa](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08366) posed the cyclic optimum. [Inal](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/C58Q9) solved the unrestricted abelian order and classified equality. [Sitjar](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24228247) used doubled labels to obtain a cyclic surplus of order \(2^n/n\) and verified the exact cyclic conjecture for \(n=8,9\); his explicit estimate can be stronger than the displayed bound at small \(n\).

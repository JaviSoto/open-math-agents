# A245212 with two odd primes, one of exponent at most four

Let $p,q$ be distinct odd primes, $a,d\ge1$, and $k\ge0$.
The theorem `OMA.SmallOriginal245212.closed` excludes the A245212 equality
at $2^k p^a q^d$ whenever $\min(a,d)\le4$.
It uses the original OEIS definition and places no size bound on either prime.

Relabel so $d\le a$ and write $K=k+1$. The argument treats the two
possible orders of the primes separately. For $d=1$, the two-adic
divisor identity gives $a\equiv2\pmod4$. When $a=2$ the six-divisor
height bound applies. For $a\ge6$ the linear signed separation supplies
the same nonzero integer used by the higher-power cofactor estimate. A
solution with $K\ge160$ would satisfy $2^K\le2^{56}K^{12}<2^K$.
In the remaining range $a\le240$ and $\min(p,q)\le5K$.

For $d=2,3,4$, a kernel-checked subdivision reduces the existing
polynomial height bound to the following maxima for $K$.

| $d$ | $a\ge2d+4, p<q$ | $a\ge2d+4, q<p$ | $a<2d+4, p<q$ | $a<2d+4, q<p$ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2 | 230 | 137 | 138 | 280 |
| 3 | 354 | 174 | 203 | 497 |
| 4 | 485 | 212 | 271 | 779 |

The two-adic identities separately force $a$ odd for $d=2$,
$a\equiv2\pmod4$ for $d=3$, and $4\mid a$ for $d=4$. The
first and last restrictions also use the theorem that the square root of
a square odd core must itself be a square at a solution.

At each remaining choice of the smaller prime, $k$, and the exponents,
leading-digit division recovers the sole possible larger prime. The
kernel then checks divisibility and the original local moment balance.
This route includes the low-divisor cases directly. It does not assume
the earlier Python search through 71 divisors.

There is a useful direct restriction on the least prime $r=\min(p,q)$:
for $1\le M\le a+1$ and $h=d+1$,
$$ (Mh-1)r\le M\bigl(2h+K(2h-1)\bigr). $$
Applied to each exponent interval, this bounds both $r$ and the range of
$K$ that must be checked for that $r$. A separate congruence, not needed
for the tables, says that $a\equiv d\pmod {16}$ whenever $k\ge2$ and
$4\mid a,d$. Indeed, modulo 8 the ratio of the weighted and ordinary
divisor sums for $p^{4j}$ is $1+2j$, and every odd residue is its own
inverse. See `SmallLeast245212.lean` and `SmallModEight245212.lean`.

The first-power table has 91,133 exponent choices, checked in both prime
orders. The tightened largest-prime tests for the other three powers have
97,578, 132,008, and 357,171 choices respectively, with the prime order
already specified.

## Reproduce

The numerical proof declarations are generated by
`python3 small_prime_blocks_245212.py d` for each of `d=2,3,4`.
From the research directory, `python3 small_degree_source_245212.py --arithmetic`
also checks the last three powers in Python and regenerates
`SmallOriginalResearch245212.lean`. That file imports only
`FormalConjectures.OEIS.«245212»`. Run the pinned `lake env lean` on the
file; the final line audits the axioms of the complete theorem.
The modular entry point is `SmallOriginal245212.lean`; the first-power
branch alone is `SmallLinearClosed245212.lean`.

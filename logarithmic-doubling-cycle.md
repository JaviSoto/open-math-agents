# A logarithmic doubling cycle forces Fonollosa's sharp bound

Let \(A\subset\mathbb Z/N\) be valid: among length-\(n\) multisets on the \(n\) distinct labels of \(A\), only \(A\) itself represents its sum. Assume some common translation of \(A\) contains a full doubling cycle \(C=\{y,2y,\ldots,2^{r-1}y\}\) of size \(r>0\), with \(2^ry=y\). Set \(B=A\setminus C\) in the translated coordinates and \(k=|B|=n-r\).

**Theorem.** Suppose \(2^r-1\ge 2(k+14)\). If \(N<2^n\), then either the translated set is doubling-closed, or \(k=1\) and its unique exterior label \(b\) satisfies \(2b=0\). In particular \(N\ge2^n-2^{\lfloor\log_2 n\rfloor}\). No paths or other structure are assumed for the exterior labels. Thus a counterexample cannot contain a doubling cycle of length at least \(\lceil\log_2(2n+29)\rceil\) for any affine doubling map.

### The exterior subset cube

We use the [established cycle absorption lemma](long-doubling-cycles.md). The subgroup generated by \(y\) has order \(2^r-1\). Write \(\pi\) for the projection modulo this subgroup. In the cyclic quotient of order \(q=N/(2^r-1)\), the following properties hold:

1. All \(2^k\) exterior subset sums \(v(S)=\pi(\sum S)\), \(S\subseteq B\), are different.
2. If a squarefree word \(T\subseteq B\) and an arbitrary multiset \(m\) on \(B\) have the same projected sum, then \(m=T\) whenever \(|m|\le|T|\). If \(|m|=|T|+1\), equality of their projected sums forces equality of their *actual* sums. These assertions follow by filling the original cycle's missing length with a suitable multiset on the cycle; see [CyclePacking.lean](CyclePacking.lean) and [CycleQuotient.lean](CycleQuotient.lean).

Let \(e=q-2^k\) be the number of residues outside this cube. Fix \(b\in B\) with \(2\pi(b)\ne0\). If \(2\pi(b)\) is represented, its representing subset has at most one element by property 2, compared with the multiset \(b,b\). It cannot be empty, and for a singleton property 2 gives \(2b\in B\) in the original group. We prove that if \(2\pi(b)\) is unrepresented, then
\[
                        2^{k-1}\le (k+14)e.
\]

### A missing successor costs many holes

Let \(X=B\setminus\{b\}\), \(l=k-1\). For \(S\subseteq X\) try to represent \(2\pi(b)+v(S)\) by an exterior subset \(T\). Such a subset, when it exists, is unique and excludes \(b\), since otherwise cancellation gives a collision between the two different cube points \(S\cup\{b\}\) and \(T\setminus\{b\}\). This defines a partial injection on the \(l\)-cube; let \(H\le e\) denote its number of missing inputs. A represented input has disjoint reduced masks \(U=S\setminus T\), \(V=T\setminus S\), with \(v(V)-v(U)=2\pi(b)\). Necessarily \(U\ne\emptyset\), as otherwise \(V\) represents the missing residue. Its gain \(d=|T|-|S|=|V|-|U|\) is at most one, by comparing \(T\) against the nonsquarefree word \(S+b+b\).

The following directional intersection rule is useful. For two different reduced masks \((U_i,V_i)\), if \(d_1\le d_2\) then \(U_1\cap V_2\ne\emptyset\). Otherwise \(V_2+U_1\) is squarefree. It has the same projected sum as \(V_1+U_2\), whose length is no greater. Property 2 forces the two multisets to agree, and cancellation of positive and negative parts makes their reduced masks identical.

For each represented \(S\) of gain \(d\), distribute mass 1 equally to the lower neighbors \(S\setminus\{u\}\), \(u\in U\). If a receiving neighbor is represented then its gain is strictly larger: its reduced mask is different since it omits \(u\), and its positive input mask, being contained in \(S\), is disjoint from \(V\). The directional rule gives the strict inequality.

Fix a receiver \(R\) and a gain \(d\in\{0,1\}\). Suppose \(a\) sources of this gain send mass to it, at the distinct points \(S_i=R\cup\{u_i\}\). The masks are different: \(u_i\in U_i\) but \(u_i\notin S_j\) when \(j\ne i\). By the rule for equal gains, \(U_j\cap V_i\ne\emptyset\). Since \(V_i\) misses \(R\), this forces \(u_j\in V_i\) for all \(j\ne i\). Thus \(|U_i|=|V_i|-d\ge a-1-d\), and also \(|U_i|\ge1\). The total mass received is at most 3 for gain 1 and at most 2 for gain 0: explicitly it is at most \(a/\max(1,a-1-d)\). Denote the corresponding source counts by \(M_1,M_0\). We have
\[
                   M_1\le3H,\qquad M_0\le2(H+M_1)\le8H.
\]
The source and image both omit exactly \(H\) masks from the same cube. Hence the sum of all gains is at least \(-lH\), by comparing both rank sums with the full cube. Each negative gain is at most \(-1\), so the number of negative sources is at most \(M_1+lH\). In total
\[
             2^l \le H+3H+8H+(3+l)H=(l+15)H=(k+14)H.
\]

### Closure in the original group

For \(k=0\) we already have closure. Otherwise \(N<2^n\) gives \(e<2^k/(2^r-1)\). Under \(2^r-1\ge2(k+14)\), a missing successor would contradict the preceding estimate. Every exterior \(b\) with \(2\pi(b)\ne0\) therefore doubles to another exterior label, with equality in the original group.

Since all projected exterior labels are distinct and nonzero, at most one can have order two in the cyclic quotient. If there is one, the [terminal lemma](CycleTerminal.lean) says it doubles into \(C\) or to zero. In the latter case no other exterior label doubles to it. The remaining exterior labels would form a doubling-closed set disjoint from the cycle, so are empty: otherwise they contain a second complete cycle of sum zero, and one can replace one cycle by a multiset of the same size on the other. For this last assertion use the shorter cycle as source of the replacement, extending its zero-sum multiset to the larger cycle's length by repeatedly splitting a cycle member into two predecessors. If the exceptional exterior label does not double to zero, the entire set is closed. The sharp conclusion now follows from the [closed-set classification](affine-doubling-classification.md) or, when \(k=1\), the [long-cycle bound](long-doubling-cycles.md).

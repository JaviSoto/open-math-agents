# A245212 through 53 odd divisors

Write $n=2^k m$ with $m$ odd, $t=\tau(m)$, and $W(b)=\sum_{d\mid b}d\tau(d)$. The question in [A245212](https://oeis.org/A245212) is whether $W(n)+\sigma(n)=2n\tau(n)$ occurs at any $m>1$. **If $t\le53$, equality holds precisely when $m=1$.** The first four-odd-prime case is $t=36$. The bounds below apply without a supplied limit on the primes, $n$, or $k$. The final exclusions are finite exact arithmetic, not a Lean proof of the full conjecture.

The [previous result](odd-divisor-count-245212.md) covers $t\le35$. Its power-of-two envelope gives $2^k\le[4(k+2)t^2]^t$ and the inequality fails permanently from $k=791$ at $t=36$. A single odd prime power cannot occur. The known [exponent obstruction](exponent-parity-245212.md) requires $2^s\mid\prod(e_i+2)$ for $s$ distinct odd primes. The [square obstruction](dyadic-power-245212.md) requires an odd part that is a square to be a fourth power. After these tests the lists in increasing order of the primes are exactly the six permutations of $(1,1,2,2)$, three of $(2,2,3)$ and two of $(2,11)$. In the four-prime cases the power of two in $\prod(e_i+2)$ is exactly $2^4$. The saturation case of the exponent obstruction requires each odd-exponent prime to be $1\bmod4$.

## Bounds on a partially specified factorization

Write $G=2^{k+1}-1$, $\delta=(k+1)/G$, $\gamma=k+\delta$. [Sahu’s dyadic inversion](https://github.com/GuillaumeBAUD-coder/open-math-agents/pull/5390) gives
$$
 (1+\gamma)t m=\gamma W(m)+\sigma(m).
$$
Let $P$ be the product of the known odd prime powers and put $A=W(P)$, $S=\sigma(P)$. A tail with exponents $e_i$ has $T=\prod_i(e_i+1)$ and $V=\prod_i\binom{e_i+2}{2}$. The normalized weighted and ordinary divisor sums of each tail power are polynomials in the reciprocal of its prime, with positive coefficients and constants $e_i+1$ and $1$ respectively. At a genuine incomplete prefix
$$
 D_\gamma=(1+\gamma)tP-\gamma AT-S>0.
$$
Putting all tail bases equal to $x$ gives a decreasing upper bound for the right side after division by the tail product, hence a finite upper bound on the next base. Fixing that base and putting all subsequent bases at infinity likewise gives a lower bound. These two bounds use integer endpoint comparisons, via doubling and binary search.

There are two inexpensive exclusions, valid at any length.

1. Put $g=\gcd((1+k2^{k+1})A,GS)$ and $u=g/\gcd(g,2^{k+1}(k+1)tP)$. Both summands of the original left side are multiples of $g$. Consequently $u$ divides the product of the unknown odd prime powers. When $u>1$, it cannot share a prime with $P$ and cannot be at most the last fixed prime; the next unknown prime is at most $u$. For example, at $k=59$ the prefix $41^2\cdot62633$ is excluded by $11$, which divides both $(1+k2^{k+1})W(P)$ and $G\sigma(P)$ but does not divide $2^{k+1}(k+1)tP$.
2. Reduction of the original equality modulo $2^{k+1}$ gives $2^{k+1}\mid W(m)-\sigma(m)$. For $m>1$ this difference is positive and strictly smaller than $W(m)$. Thus an entire box of prime bases with upper bound $W(m)\le2^{k+1}$ is impossible. To get such an upper bound at an *unknown* but boxed prefix use $D_k=(1+k)tP-kAT-S$. Since $AT\ge tP$, $D_k\ge D_\gamma>0$, and $D_k$ is integral. If $A\le A_+$ and $2(k+1)A_+T\le G$, then $D_\gamma=D_k-\delta(AT-tP)\ge1/2$. For minimum remaining base $x$, every nonconstant monomial in the tail reciprocal polynomials is at most $1/x$. The sums of their nonconstant coefficients are $V-T$ and $T-1$. Hence for $S\le S_+$,
$$
 x\le 2\{(k+1)A_+(V-T)+S_+(T-1)\}.
$$
This propagates upper bounds to the next prefix. When the hypothesis for $D_\gamma\ge1/2$ fails the checker simply does not use this exclusion. A sharper first upper bound follows by putting a boxed prime at its *lowest* endpoint in the monotone comparison for the subsequent prime. Each later multiplication uses the *highest* endpoint to bound the unnormalized moments.

## Eliminating one of the last two primes

Let $g_0=\gcd(k+1,G)$, $Q=G/g_0$ and $H=kQ+(k+1)/g_0$. The integers $Q,H$ are coprime. Every solution with $m>1$ has the same positive integer $h$ in
$$
 W(m)-tm=hQ,\qquad tm-\sigma(m)=hH .
$$
Suppose one of the last two exponents equals one. Call its prime $x$ and call the other power $y^d$, independently of their ordering. Write $J=tP$, $W_y=A\sum_{j=0}^d(j+1)y^j$ and $Z_y=S\sum_{j=0}^d y^j$. The two equalities are linear in $x$:
$$
 (2W_y-Jy^d)x=hQ-W_y,\qquad (Jy^d-Z_y)x=hH+Z_y.
$$
Elimination gives the necessary polynomial equation
$$
 h\big[(Q+H)Jy^d-QZ_y-2HW_y\big]
 +Jy^d(Z_y-W_y)-W_y Z_y=0.                         \tag{1}
$$
Its degree is $2d$: the leading coefficient of the last two terms is $J[S-(d+1)A]-(d+1)AS<0$, because $A\ge S>0$. For every integer root $y$ in range, a prospective solution must have $Jy^d-Z_y>0$ and $x=(hH+Z_y)/(Jy^d-Z_y)$. Both integrality and original prime ordering are tested. Doubt about primality cannot discard a genuine root: the checker stops even on a surviving composite pair.

Box the *ordered* smaller remaining prime in $[L,U]$ where the lower-limit deficit after adjoining it is positive at $L$. With that prime temporarily fixed at $z$, put $A'=W(Pz^a)$, $S'=\sigma(Pz^a)$ (local-polynomial multiplication, without any assumption that $z$ is prime), and let $d'$ be the ordered final exponent. Set
$$
 D=(1+\gamma)tPz^a-(d'+1)\gamma A'-S',\qquad B=d'\gamma A'+S'.
$$
The [leading-digit identity](odd-divisor-count-245212.md) forces the last prime to equal $\lceil B/D\rceil$ when a solution exists (if $d'>1$ and $B/D$ is integral there is no candidate). Divide numerator and denominator of $B/D$ by $z^a$: the former decreases and the latter increases. Thus the last prime lies between $\max(L,\lceil B(U)/D(U)\rceil)$ and $\lceil B(L)/D(L)\rceil$. On the resulting rectangle, $W(m)-tm$ is increasing in both bases: expand the local polynomials; all coefficients are nonnegative since their leading term already contributes at least $tm$. Endpoints bound $h$. In addition, $h$ is divisible by
$$
 \operatorname{lcm}\left(\frac{\gcd(A,J)}{\gcd(A,J,Q)},\
                        \frac{\gcd(S,J)}{\gcd(S,J,H)}\right).
$$
This follows from the two surplus equalities before eliminating $x$.

On boxes where there are relatively few values of $h$, trial small prime moduli remove values having no root of (1) modulo that prime. All residue classes of $y$, including zero, are considered. For each surviving $h$ the program finds all integer roots as follows. For an interval $[l,u]$, form the coefficients of
$$
 (1+z)^{2d}f\big((l+uz)/(1+z)\big),
$$
where $f$ is (1). If its coefficients all have one weak sign there is no interior root: at $z>0$ the polynomial is strictly of that sign. The polynomial is nonzero and both endpoints are checked separately. Otherwise bisect, eventually checking each integer in unresolved intervals of length at most two. This rule needs no floating-point root approximation.

Other boxes are exhausted by segmented sieving of the penultimate prime. For the last prime we use the exact leading-digit rule, require divisibility by the constant coefficient, then test the original equality. Heuristics choose when to split a box and when to switch methods; neither outcome exempts a box from a mathematical test.

## Replay and coverage

Place [odd_tau36_245212.py](odd_tau36_245212.py) and its unchanged [base arithmetic](odd_divisor_count_245212.py) in the same directory. All dependencies are in the Python standard library.

    python3 odd_tau36_245212.py --self-test
    python3 odd_tau36_245212.py --t 36 --through 53 --audit --jobs 4

The self-test compares the segmented sieve with trial division, checks 500 polynomials with planted roots against exhaustive evaluation, and recovers both coordinates in 72 positive examples constructed to satisfy the surplus identities. Each search prints the ordered profile and its entire inclusive range of $k$. Use e.g. `--t 36 --exps 2 1 1 2 --k 129 129` to check a single difficult case. The `--plan` option estimates coverage without performing the terminal checks; it is not a verification run.
